# What to do: the box's safety sheet

Short, plain guidance the "ask the box" models answer from (cloud Nemotron and the on-board model).
Written in our own words from the public advice of Ready.gov (FEMA), the National Weather Service and USGS.
The runner sends only the sections that match what the box heard (`## CODE ...` headings), plus GENERAL.
Keep every section short: the on-board model reads it on the Pi CPU.

## GENERAL
- If anyone is hurt, trapped, or in danger, call 911 yourself. The box never calls 911.
- Follow instructions from local officials and NOAA Weather Radio; they know the details for your area.
- Keep your phone charged, shoes on, and a flashlight close. Check on neighbors who live alone.
- Never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors or in a garage: carbon monoxide kills without warning.

## EQW EARTHQUAKE QUAKE
- During shaking: DROP to your hands and knees, COVER your head and neck under a sturdy table or next to an inside wall, HOLD ON until the shaking stops.
- Stay inside. Do not run outside or stand in a doorway. Stay away from windows and tall furniture.
- In bed: stay there and cover your head with a pillow.
- After: expect aftershocks, and drop, cover and hold on again each time.
- Check yourself and others for injuries. Put on sturdy shoes because of broken glass.
- If you smell gas or hear hissing: leave the building, do not use flames or light switches, and call the gas company or 911 from outside.
- Stay out of buildings that look damaged. Text instead of calling, so phone lines stay free for emergencies.

## TOR TOA TORNADO EWW
- Warning: go now to the lowest floor, to a small room with no windows (closet, bathroom, inner hallway). Cover your head and neck.
- A mobile home or car is not safe: get to a sturdy building.
- Do not wait to see the tornado. Stay sheltered until the warning ends.
- Watch: a tornado is possible. Know where you will shelter and keep the radio on.

## FFW FFA FLW FLA FLOOD
- Move to higher ground now. Do not wait for instructions.
- Turn around, don't drown: never walk or drive through flood water. Moving water a few inches deep can knock you down, and about a foot of it can float a car.
- Stay away from creeks and low water crossings. If water is rising inside, go to the highest floor, not a closed attic.

## SVR SVA HWW HWA THUNDERSTORM WIND
- Go indoors and stay away from windows. When thunder roars, go indoors.
- Avoid corded phones, plumbing and electrical equipment during lightning.
- Strong wind can bring down trees and power lines: stay away from fallen lines and report them.

## WSW BZW WINTER COLD
- Stay inside and dress in layers. Close off unused rooms to keep heat in.
- Let faucets drip to keep pipes from freezing.
- Never heat the home with an oven, grill or generator indoors.

## HEAT EHW
- Get to a cooler place: air conditioning, the lowest floor, or a cooling center.
- Drink water, rest, and cool your skin with a wet cloth.
- Confusion, hot dry skin, or passing out can be heat stroke: call 911.

## FRW FIRE SMOKE
- Get out and stay out. Crawl low under smoke. Feel doors before opening; a hot door means use another way out.
- Once outside, call 911. Never go back in.

## EVI EVACUATION
- Leave when officials tell you to. Take medicines, documents, phone chargers, water and pets.
- Use the routes officials give. Do not drive around barricades.

## SPW HMW CEM LAE SHELTER
- Shelter in place: go inside, close and lock windows and doors, turn off fans and systems that pull in outside air.
- Go to an inner room and keep the radio on until officials say it is safe.

## EAN NIC NPT NATIONAL
- A national alert: stay calm, keep the radio on, and follow the instructions given.

## FALL
- If you fell: do not get up fast. Check for pain first. If your neck, back or hip hurts, or you cannot move, stay still and call for help.
- If you can move: roll to your side, get to your hands and knees, crawl to a sturdy chair and push up slowly.
