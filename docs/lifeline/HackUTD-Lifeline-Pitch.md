# Lifeline Grid: Pitch, Demo, Q&A, Video, Devpost

Goal of this note: win the room. Judges spend about 5 minutes per table and rank teams pairwise, so they remember one story, one moment and one number. Ours: **a real Texan died when her oxygen machine lost power. We turn her lost hours into a countdown and a phone call.**

Rule for every claim below: if we did not build it tonight, say "simulated" out loud. Judges forgive simulation. They punish being caught overclaiming.

---

## 1. The story

### The hook (local, true, sourced)
- **Winter Storm Uri, Feb 2021.** Texas DSHS final count: **246 deaths** across 77 counties. **25** were from worsened chronic illness, including loss of power to oxygen and other electricity-dependent equipment. Source: [DSHS mortality surveillance report (PDF)](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/news/updates/SMOC_FebWinterStorm_MortalitySurvReport_12-30-21.pdf), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/01/03/1069974416/texas-winter-storm-final-death-toll).
- **Right here in DFW:** Dallas County lost 22 people, Tarrant 11 ([WFAA](https://wfaa.com/article/news/local/the-ones-we-lost-at-least-22-people-in-dallas-county-alone-lost-their-lives-to-the-february-2021-winter-storm/287-a8654006-deb6-434b-8751-b60cb9eaf55e)). A family sued Oncor after Katherine Birdwell died struggling for air when her oxygen machine stopped without power ([Dallas Morning News](https://www.dallasnews.com/2021/03/03/families-sue-oncor-after-deaths-of-man-from-hypothermia-woman-who-couldnt-use-oxygen-machine-during-winter-storm/)). In the Fort Worth area, oxygen needs drove most of 50+ MedStar calls in the outage ([Undark](https://undark.org/2021/05/11/power-outages-medical-equipment/)).
- **It happened again. Hurricane Beryl, July 2024, Houston.** 2.2M CenterPoint customers out in triple-digit heat index. At least 10 Texas deaths tied to heat or failed medical equipment after the outage. Mark Rutland, 64, of Webster died of hyperthermia when his oxygen concentrator shut off ([Wikipedia summary with citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_Hurricane_Beryl_in_Texas), [ABC13/AP](https://abc13.com/post/hurricane-beryl-related-deaths-texas-rise-36/15096054/), [Texas Standard](https://www.texasstandard.org/stories/hurricane-beryl-deaths-houston-area-seniors-heat-centerpoint-outage/)).
- **Scale:** about **237,692** Texas Medicare beneficiaries rely on electricity-dependent medical equipment (June 2023 HHS emPOWER data, per [KHOU](https://www.khou.com/article/news/investigations/texans-power-outage-map/285-437f1ff7-5cd1-4407-b5a5-6845803cacdc)). Harris County has the most in Texas. Nationally over 3M Medicare beneficiaries have a claim for this kind of equipment ([HHS emPOWER](https://empowerprogram.hhs.gov/about.html)).
- **The gap today:** Texas utilities keep "critical care" lists, but the designation **does not guarantee power or priority restoration**. Oncor said it **could not prioritize** these customers during Uri ([NBC DFW](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/nbc-5-responds/is-there-a-way-to-prioritize-medically-fragile-texans-in-a-power-disaster/2590136/?amp=1), [ABC13](https://abc13.com/post/priority-designated-customers-chronic-illness-taking-care-elderly/15054977/)). The list says *where* people are. Nobody knows *how many hours they have left* or *whether they are okay*.

Use one name, one number, one gap. Do not stack all of these in the live pitch. Uri + Birdwell + "nobody knows how many hours they have left" is enough.

### One-sentence problem statement (names the sponsor challenge)
> "On the worst day, at scale, the people who die first are the ones whose oxygen or ventilator runs on a battery nobody is watching. Lifeline Grid finds who needs help most urgently, by the minute, so no one is left out."

(This mirrors State Farm's brief word for word: "find who needs help most urgently, coordinate assistance, leave no one out" from [HackUTD-Tracks](../tracks/HackUTD-Tracks.md).)

### 15-second opener (memorize)
> "February 2021. Power's out across Texas. A woman in North Texas is on home oxygen. Her machine stops. She dies struggling for air. The utility had a list of people like her. It said where she lived. It didn't say she had two hours left. We built the thing that knows."

## 2. State Farm angle

### Why an insurer cares
- **Precedent they already run:** State Farm gives eligible Texas homeowners a free **Ting** sensor from Whisker Labs that watches home electrical power and has prevented thousands of fires. They invested in Whisker Labs, and Whisker shares only **summary counts** of events with State Farm, not raw data ([State Farm newsroom](https://newsroom.statefarm.com/state-farm-ting-electrical-fire/), [Ting FAQ](https://www.tingfire.com/statefarm-faqs/), [PropertyCasualty360](https://propertycasualty360.com/2024/04/24/state-farms-ting-fire-monitor-detected-and-mitigated-over-500-fires-in-2022/?amp=1)). **This is the single best line for a State Farm judge:** "This is Ting for the people on life support. Same model: free plug-in device, opt-in, the insurer sees counts, not people."
- **CAT response:** Uri produced 456K+ Texas claims and about $8.2B expected insured losses ([Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/southcentral/2021/09/24/633569.htm), [Insurance Journal, $5.8B paid](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southcentral/2022/01/25/650780.htm)). State Farm alone saw 18,900+ frozen-pipe claims in days ([Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/southcentral/2021/02/19/601888.htm)). Their CAT teams already have to decide where to go first. Our ranked map is that decision, for people.
- **Loss prevention = people first:** a death in a covered home is the worst outcome for the family and for the agent who knows them. Prevention is cheaper than any claim.
- **Good Neighbor / community:** State Farm has backed the Red Cross for 30+ years and is a Red Cross Mission Leader ([Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/about-us/news-and-events/press-release/2026/red-cross-honors-state-farm--with-the-mission-leader-award.html), [State Farm disaster response](https://www.statefarm.com/about-us/community/safety-awareness/disaster-response)). Our escalation list is exactly what a Red Cross or county responder wants handed to them.
- **Agents know their policyholders:** 19K-ish local agents are the "trusted voice." Our call script can say "This is a check-in from your State Farm agent's safety program." (Say this is a possible deployment, not a deal.)

### How to phrase it
- "Who needs help most urgently, and in what order" (their words).
- "A free safety device, like Ting, that protects people, not just property."
- "Opt-in. The person and their caregiver own the data. Responders see only what they need in an emergency."

### What NOT to say
- Never "insurers can use this to price risk", "flag high-risk policyholders", "adjust premiums" or anything about denying or limiting claims.
- Never "monitor customers" or "see inside homes." Radar is presence and motion only, no images, and it runs on the device.
- Never claim State Farm, Oncor, or any county is a partner. Say "could plug into."
- Don't say "replace 911" or "replace the utility critical-care list." Say "fills the gap between the list and the rescue."
- Don't call it a medical device or claim it diagnoses anything. It watches power and presence.

## 3. NVIDIA angle: "beyond a chatbot" in 30 seconds

The track asks for: not a chatbot, a multi-step agent that plans and executes, uses external tools/APIs, solves a real problem ([HackUTD-Tracks](../tracks/HackUTD-Tracks.md), [HackUTD-Research-Track-NVIDIA](../tracks/HackUTD-Research-Track-NVIDIA.md)).

Make it undeniable on screen:
1. **No chat box anywhere.** The agent is triggered by events (a node reports power loss), not by a human typing.
2. **Visible trace panel** on the dashboard, one line per step, live:
   - `PLAN` "Grid down in zone 75080. 37 homes on battery. Rank by minutes-to-harm."
   - `TOOL rank_homes()` returns the sorted list
   - `TOOL call_home(#12)` (Twilio) ... `RESULT no answer`
   - `TOOL radar_status(#12)` returns "present, no motion for 9 min"
   - `DECIDE` escalate #12 to responder, priority 1
   - `TOOL notify_responder()` ... `VERIFY` re-poll in 2 min
   - `REPLAN` battery drain faster than forecast, #31 moved up
3. **Verify step is the killer detail.** Agents that check their own results are what "beyond a chatbot" means. Show at least one case where the agent's first action failed (no answer) and it chose a different tool.
4. **Multi-model, say it once:** Nemotron plans and calls tools; a small model (or plain code) does the math for the battery forecast; ElevenLabs voice speaks; the agent writes the responder hand-off. Name which Nemotron model and where it runs (local on GPU vs. NVIDIA API) and only claim what's true.
5. **30-second line:** "Nobody chats with this. A power reading wakes it up. It plans, calls tools, checks the results, and re-plans every two minutes as batteries drain. That's the trace on the right."

## 4. Judge Q&A (12 hardest, crisp answers)

1. **Why not just use utility data?** Utilities know where critical-care customers live and whether the grid is down. They don't know how much battery is left or if the person is okay, and Oncor said it couldn't prioritize them in Uri ([NBC DFW](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/nbc-5-responds/is-there-a-way-to-prioritize-medically-fragile-texans-in-a-power-disaster/2590136/?amp=1)). We'd ingest the utility outage map as an input, not replace it.
2. **Privacy / HIPAA?** We don't store diagnoses. We store device type, battery runtime and "present / moving / no motion." Opt-in, consented, owned by the person and caregiver. HIPAA applies to providers, health plans and their contractors; a deployment through one of them would sign a business associate agreement. Insurers get counts only, like the Ting model.
3. **False alarms?** Two signals must agree before we escalate: battery low AND (no phone answer OR no motion). A call goes out first; a human responder is the last step, not the first. The cost of one extra call is small next to the cost of a miss.
4. **Who installs the node?** Plug-in, no wiring: it sits between the backup battery and the device. The person, a family member, the DME supplier who delivers the oxygen concentrator, or an agent at a home visit. Same install model as Ting.
5. **Cost per home?** Parts estimate around $30 to $40 at hobby prices (ESP32, current sensor, 24 GHz mmWave module, LoRa radio). State the BOM we actually used tonight with real prices. Compare to the cost of one ambulance run.
6. **What if the person doesn't answer?** That's the point of the ranking. No answer + low battery + no motion = escalate to the caregiver, then a responder, with address, device, minutes left and the last radar reading. Re-tries are scheduled by the agent.
7. **How accurate is the battery estimate?** We measure voltage and current on the battery line and project runtime from the actual draw, not the label. Give the error band we actually measured tonight, and say the agent always plans on the pessimistic end.
8. **Why radar, not a camera?** Privacy and darkness. mmWave sees presence, motion and breathing-scale movement without any image, works in the dark during an outage, and nothing that looks like a person ever leaves the device.
9. **Why LoRa?** Cell towers lose power too; in Uri and Beryl, cell service degraded. LoRa runs for days on a small battery and reaches kilometers per hop, so nodes can relay to a gateway that still has backhaul. Be honest about what we showed live (one or two nodes) vs. designed.
10. **Does it scale?** The simulated 500-home city shows the ranking and agent loop at scale. The agent's job grows with the number of homes *at risk right now*, not total homes, because we only act on the top of the list. Texas alone has about 237K people on this equipment.
11. **Business model?** Insurer-funded free device like Ting; Medicare Advantage plans and DME suppliers (they already deliver the equipment); counties and utilities as a paid dashboard for emergency management. We don't sell data.
12. **What did you actually build in 24 hours vs. simulate?** Answer with a clean split, no hedging. Built: [fill in tonight, e.g. working node measuring a real battery + radar presence, Nemotron agent loop, live phone call, dashboard]. Simulated: the 499 other homes, the responder side, city-wide LoRa mesh. Say it before they ask.

Bonus if asked "isn't this just an alert button?" A medical alert button needs the person to press it. People in trouble often can't. Ours notices on its own.

## 7. Devpost write-up skeleton

**Title options:** Lifeline Grid (current) · Breathing Room (recommended, see section 8) · Last Watt

**Tagline:** "When the grid goes down, we know who has the least time left and we call them first."

**Inspiration**
For the State Farm "Worst Day, at Scale" challenge: in Winter Storm Uri, 25 of Texas's 246 storm deaths came from worsened illness, including people whose oxygen and medical equipment lost power ([DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/news/updates/SMOC_FebWinterStorm_MortalitySurvReport_12-30-21.pdf)). It happened again in Hurricane Beryl. Utility critical-care lists say where these people live, not how long their battery lasts or whether they're okay.

**What it does**
Finds who needs help most urgently and makes sure no one is left out (State Farm challenge). A low-cost node in each home measures backup battery runtime and uses mmWave radar for presence and motion. A Nemotron agent ranks every home by minutes-to-harm, calls people, escalates the silent ones to responders, and re-ranks as batteries drain. LoRa keeps nodes talking when cell service fails.

**How we built it**
- Hardware (Best Hardware): ESP32, voltage/current sensing on the battery line, 24 GHz mmWave radar, LoRa radio. [List exact parts.]
- Agent (NVIDIA "Use Nemotron Beyond a Chatbot"): event-triggered, multi-step plan/act/verify loop with tool calls to Twilio, ElevenLabs, radar queries and responder notifications. No chat interface. [Model name, where it ran.]
- Data (MLH Tiger Data, if used): battery and presence readings as time series; drain-rate forecasting from history.
- Dashboard and 500-home city simulation.

**Challenges we ran into** [Fill in truthfully: e.g. estimating runtime from a noisy current reading, radar false "empty room", call reliability on venue Wi-Fi.]

**Accomplishments that we're proud of** [The live moment: flip the switch, phone rings within N seconds. Give the measured number.]

**What we learned** [One hardware lesson, one agent lesson.]

**What's next** Pilot with a DME supplier or county emergency office; ingest the utility outage map and HHS emPOWER ZIP-level counts for planning; insurer-funded free device in the Ting model; multi-hop LoRa field test.

## 8. Name check

**"Lifeline Grid" as an exact name:** no product found with that exact name ([search](https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Lifeline+Grid%22) turned up only generic "lifeline infrastructure" and FEMA "Community Lifelines").

**But "Lifeline" has two bad collisions with our exact audience:**
- **Lifeline medical alert** (formerly Philips Lifeline, sold to Connect America in 2020) is a personal emergency button for seniors, the same buyer and use case ([Telecare Aware](https://telecareaware.com/connect-america-acquires-philips-aging-and-caregiving-including-lifeline/), [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/connect-america-looks-senior-care-purchase-royal-philips-aging-and-caregiving-business)). A judge may think we cloned it.
- **FCC Lifeline** is the federal phone subsidy for low-income people, many of whom are our users. Confusing on a call ("This is Lifeline...").
- FEMA uses "Community Lifelines" as an official term. Fine to reference, not great to name after.

**Alternatives (quick conflict check, not a trademark search):**
| Name | Fit | Conflict check |
|---|---|---|
| **Breathing Room** | Time margin + oxygen; warm, human, easy to say on a call | No product in medical/outage space found; "Breathe" HR software and "Breathe Crisis Response" app exist but different names. Generic phrase, so weak trademark. |
| **Last Watt** | Battery countdown, memorable, hardware-y | No exact-name product found; only unrelated "Watt" energy apps. Slightly grim for a call voice. |
| **Vigil** | Watching over someone | **Conflict:** Vigil Health Solutions makes senior-living call systems ([LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/vigil-health-solutions-2/)). Avoid. |
| **KeepWatch** | Watching | **Conflict:** existing KeepWatch Apple Watch health app and a KeepWatch AI project ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/keepwatch/id6504386253)). Avoid. |
| **Runway** | Minutes of runway left | **Conflict:** Runway is a major AI company. Confusing at an AI track. Avoid. |

**Recommendation: Breathing Room.** It says the product (we buy people time), it works as a voice line ("This is Breathing Room, checking on you"), it avoids the Lifeline medical-alert collision, and it reads as care, not surveillance, which matters for the State Farm judge. Backup: Last Watt. If the team prefers to keep the current name, drop "Lifeline" from the call script at minimum. Decision is Rafael and Godfrey's.
